## Numerical, ownership, policy and stop contract

The documented ordering agrees with src/simplex_start.jl:55: default workspace initialization and reconstruction precede driver-policy installation, copied supplied basis admission, bound checks, checkpoint invalidation, imported refactor/reconstruction, finite/reliability checks and final devex reset. Default factorization and diagnostic work can therefore occur before an invalid supplied basis is rejected. The workspace retains the input problem; copying the two basis vectors and concatenating bounds does not deep-copy the model or every scalar object.

The supplied progress policy governs the earlier default initialization, including refactorization configuration. The separate policy keyword is installed afterward. _install_driver_policy! retains the clock, offsets and referenced objective/scaling/diagnostics; it neither clones diagnostics nor reruns initial refactorization configuration. Arbitrary callbacks can mutate or retain objects, so no whole-function rollback, observer-neutrality or exclusive graph ownership follows.

The native default disables solve refinement and recovery even for adaptive strategy. _checked_basis_solve! performs its base solve before optional refinement, and reliable refinement may return before polling stop. Imported recompute receives the explicit guarded callback; default recompute uses the clock-only fallback. They are not automatically OR-combined. Recovery checkpoint admission can poll before quality/copy and after its observer. True can decline checkpoint/refinement work without universally imposing initializer cancellation; a throwing _StopCallback preserves the original exception by rethrow. No actual callback occurrence or wall-clock expiry is newly demonstrated.

The BigFloat start wrapper scans stored matrix/objective/finite-bound precision and combines it with ambient and solve-tolerance precision. It restores the ambient precision on unwind. Keyword-default construction occurs before that wrapper; typed option tolerances retain stored values and time_limit converts to Float64. This is distinct from precision recovery and does not justify a universal precision claim for every option. Reliable reconstructed equations do not imply primal/dual feasibility, forward accuracy, pivot safety or original-model certification.
