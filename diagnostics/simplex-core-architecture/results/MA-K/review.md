## Routing and numerical correctness

The actual helper and caller support the proposed narrow route. `LinearProblem{Rational{BigInt}}` plus `Vector{Tuple{Int,Rational{BigInt}}}` is the ordinary rational row representation from `_row_entries`; other types and containers continue to the generic method. The explicit `invoke` tuple selects the generic signature for non-eight lengths rather than recursively selecting the specialized method. The plan's `::Bool` signature notation describes the return contract: the current source does not annotate its return type, and its exact generic source must be retained as required by Task1.

The caller currently permits 2–8 nonzero terms, fill estimate at most 10 and cumulative estimated updates at most 200000. The proposed selector does not raise those caps. Original row position, including the pivot position, matters: two eligible endpoint additions can occur at one position, but initial assignments and fixed shared-activity reuse cannot count. With a pivot among the first three positions, four or more nonpivot terms remain after position three. Their cost is not known, so this is a speculative amortization cutoff, not a proof of savings.

The finite-pivot, live-endpoint, unequal finite current-bound, nonzero and unequal-denominator requirements are concrete and implementable in the existing ordered branches. Testing signs and the two numerator sizes at the second eligible addition, then permanently declining on failure, avoids an open-ended classifier. Both numerator signs must agree; negative values are compatible with the public base-two digit count. The installed public `ndigits` path reaches `MPZ.sizeinbase` for BigInt/base2 without a source-level absolute-value or shift temporary. This source fact does not prove zero native cost. Thresholds and denominator families must be frozen before runtime results; the plan requires offline correction/review if a designated target cannot satisfy the fixed predicate.

Products, exact conversions, summation order, final bound decisions, zero assignment and shared fixed-activity shortcuts stay unchanged. Finite tight-bound oracles, both signs, all pivot positions, infinities before/after selection, 128/129/130-bit boundaries, failed selector cases, zero/cancellation and shared-prefix divergence are specifically required. Custom containers and BigFloat stored/ambient precision mismatches exercise fallback behavior. The 256-fixture cap is not an exhaustive Cartesian product: MA-L must publish the concrete feasible coverage and exclusions, as the plan requires. No candidate or fixture inventory exists yet to certify that coverage.

## Ownership and retained memory

The all-nonzero-additions rule after activation is essential. Returning to the original same-denominator constructor could let a new rational share a mutable pool denominator; excluding only the endpoint objects would then fail to protect that leaf. The plan explicitly forbids this mixture. Before activation, ordinary prefix values are input-owned or ordinary arithmetic results; the new pool is disjoint. After activation, each live endpoint is either outside the pool or exactly a pool destination. Three destinations always leave one distinct from the two current endpoints, including between minimum and maximum updates. Products remain outside the pool; unchanged zero and fixed reuse branches preserve that invariant. Zero results followed by reuse and shared input leaves still require the mandated runtime ownership tests.

The inspected MA-J helper copies equal denominators into the destination and normalizes exactly; buffered general addition uses three independent BigInt buffers and normalizes. Three rationals plus those three buffers mean nine distinct mutable leaves, not nine fixed-size allocations. Size grows with intermediate integer limbs. The pool stops mutating before final comparisons and only Bool escapes. No persistent cache, model retention or update-chain storage is proposed; temporary limb capacity may remain until GC. Native frees and arbitrary exception/OOM equivalence are not established.

## Work, schedule and acceptance

Frequency is each equality-bound check, potentially once per candidate pivot of an eligible row. Typed short fallback adds dispatch/length/invoke work. Eight-term calls add position/state branches and early scalar eligibility checks; at most two base2 size queries occur once. Bound equality may require an extra shared comparison and must be accounted for. Selected calls pay fresh scratch setup and up to four endpoint identity comparisons per private addition, plus MA normalization and deliberate private same-denominator work. There is no new full-vector/matrix scan, basis solve, factorization, conversion pass or input copy. Scalar work scales with integer limb size and existing row additions, not with basis-update-chain length.

Independent count check: 24 targets + 12 short + 9 ordinary + 4 adversarial + 27 generic = 76 cases, including 52 controls. Per process, 76 × 10 pairs × 2 sides gives 1520 windows: 456 warm and 1064 measured. At 128 complete calls/window that is 194560 calls/process; three processes give 4560 windows and 583680 calls. Three inclusive 120-second process caps total 360 seconds, each with a 114-second run guard and six-second shutdown reserve. First-process correctness/RED/GREEN and import/initialization must fit that cap; exact stage commands remain a later admission deliverable. A timeout stops the sequence without automatic retry.

Whole-call timing includes routing and fresh scratch, with separate initialization/compilation records and zero measured compile/recompile required. Alternating pairs, reversed starting side, three processes, preserved GC/outliers, per-process allocation gates and per-case rejection avoid hiding a losing workload in pooled success. Every target requires <=0.95 pooled ratio, 18/21 faster pairs, each process below 1 and lower bytes/events; every control requires pooled ratio <=1, each process <=1.02 and no increased median bytes/events. The strict controls can reject the entire hypothesis, including activated cheap tails. An eight-term zero/unit/fixed/cancelling tail is not assumed profitable merely because its prefix qualifies. Unbounded-after-selection behavior has correctness coverage; performance coverage remains the explicitly bounded grid, not all possible rows.

Unnecessary work/allocation verdict: no production work or memory is introduced by this documentation-only change. The planned selector incurs real, potentially unnecessary overhead, and selected false positives may allocate needlessly. The plan properly leaves that UNQUALIFIED and requires control rejection rather than asserting zero overhead. The mandatory private same-denominator work is justified by ownership correctness. No missing work/memory gate warrants rejecting this plan. Current MA-J evidence independently recomputes to an eight-term rational-general ratio of 0.88486005 with lower bytes/events, but four-term rational units/cancellation ratios of about 1.2319/1.5070 and 368 extra bytes/call. Those observations motivate selection without qualifying this new selector.
