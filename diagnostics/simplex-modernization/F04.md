# F04: Pivot validation, reselection and atomic application

The adaptive profile adds `pivot_validation`, independently switchable for
ablations. It checks independent row and column estimates against componentwise
basis-solve residuals, dot-product rounding and a separate pivot-error limit.
Accepted pivots have a consistent sign and remain separated from the estimated
error. A small accurate pivot can pass even below the old absolute cutoff;
unrepresentable reciprocals or update coefficients cannot pass.

Candidate solves receive bounded residual corrections when needed. The existing
additional dual row/direction/price checks remain in place. F05 will extract the
correction kernel for reuse by every basis solve. Residual contractions bound
disagreement between estimates; they are not claimed as a general forward-error
certificate for an ill-conditioned basis.

Rejected dual candidates trigger another entering variable and then another
violated row. Primal selection tries another leaving row within the Harris
limit, then another entering variable. Candidate lists belong to the current
attempted basis, are bounded by the policy, and are cleared after refresh or
completion. Exhaustion produces a numerical outcome, never an infeasibility or
optimality proof based on excluded candidates.

Candidate workspaces own their values, costs, bounds, basis and pricing arrays.
They reuse typed caches and share the live factor until the final update. The
deadline is checked immediately before that update. No callback runs between
the factor update and the complete live-state copy. A failed backend update
rebuilds the old live basis before propagating failure or retrying. Completion
observers and post-step refactorization run after the state is committed.
Thus a callback failure before application counts no step; one after application
sees a consistent completed step. Actual refactorization work remains counted.

Primal candidate values are checked before the factor update, including exact
nonbasic bound values after flips. The ordinary full primal recomputation then
finishes in the callback-free window before commit, so observers see reduced
costs for the new basis as well. Optional refactorization remains after commit.
Every primal step still performs a full recomputation; skipping it remains
F08/F09.

## Validation

The final full production suite passed **225,957/225,957** checks (13m17.6s,
exit 0), and the development suite passed **624/624** checks (exit 0), including
all 26 JET inference checks. Focused numerical-storage and pivot verification
passed 2,013 checks before the final atomicity correction; the corrected pivot
run passed 438/438 checks. External comparisons and replay also passed.
Focused development included independent pivot checks, stale factors under
both algorithms and all
four updates/two backends, real cancellation-driven alternative selection,
callback/logger failures, bounded corrections, rejected-candidate budgets,
small pivots across scalar types, and finite update range checks.

The callback review caught a real staging error: a shared factor had changed
before the live basis, and a stop callback could observe the mismatch. The
reproducer failed three assertions before moving the deadline check and
post-step maintenance outside that transition. The corrected test passed.
Additional failing regressions covered cached policy/time context, a bound flip
from -1e16 to 1, and unrepresentable factor-update coefficients.

The first full-suite run found four numeric-storage contract violations in the
new caches; the unchanged recursive storage audit rejected them. Concrete typed
buffers replaced those fields. The first development run also found inference
loss through heterogeneous field-copy loops, captured mutable retry flags and
redundant internal observer wrapping. Explicit field copies, immutable attempt
flags and direct internal forwarding fixed those findings. Diagnostic counters
and timers belong to the live solve; completion events are recorded once after
commit, and rejected attempts retain the cost of work actually performed.

Verification uses Julia 1.13 and the local offline dependency cache:

```sh

julia --startup-file=no --project=dev dev/tests/runtests.jl
```

The typed-cache revision passed 225,953 production checks and 624 development
checks. A subsequent callback regression exposed stale primal reduced costs
at commit (two failing assertions). Moving the existing full recomputation
before publication fixes that gap. Final verification uses the revised source.

## External comparison and performance limits

The F03 adaptive parent (`d2b23d5`), current legacy, adaptive with
`pivot_validation = false`, and full adaptive profiles each completed **112/112
OPTIMAL** samples: eight frozen NetLib/MIPLib cases, both algorithms, seven
repetitions, 60-second per-solve limit. MIPLib models were explicit LP
relaxations. Every sample passed original-model primal validation. Adaptive
objectives agreed with the parent within `max(1e-7, 1e-9*abs(objective))`.
The ablation exactly matched the fresh parent in objectives, iterations and
refactorizations; current legacy exactly matched F03 legacy observations.

Full adaptive validation is substantially more expensive on several cases.
The geometric mean of per-case median elapsed-time ratios against F03 adaptive
was **2.045 for primal** and **2.287 for dual**. The ablation ratios were 0.961
and 1.007 respectively. These are warmed, short-solve measurements; they do not
establish large-model performance or a speedup.

The largest regression is adlittle dual: its median increased from 0.0028983 s
to 0.217731898 s (75.124x), with 6 -> 73 refactorizations. Adlittle primal also
became 14.904x slower despite fewer completed iterations. These costs are a
measured limitation of this conservative intermediate stage. The default remains
legacy; F05/F06 refinement and recovery work must address repeated repair work,
and F25 must reassess performance before choosing defaults. No tolerance or
certificate was weakened to hide this regression.

A separate one-sample diagnosed adlittle run retained the same iteration and
refactor counts. Primal recorded 28 rejected pivots, 28 pivot refactor events,
36 correction attempts and 8 accepted corrections. Dual recorded 139 rejected
pivots, 72 pivot refactor events, 189 correction attempts and 47 accepted
corrections, plus one failed certification before recovery. Both final results
were OPTIMAL and passed original primal validation. This census identifies
repeated validation/recovery work; it is not an isolated timing attribution.
As in the F01 diagnosed adlittle baseline, the diagnostic complementarity
metric is infinite; the JSON retains it as the string "inf". This run is not
an independent original-model dual certificate.

## Repair replay

A fresh diagnosed legacy pk1 solve captured its first actual repair state with
the final source format. Replaying it with both `stable_ratio = true` and
`pivot_validation = true` and a fresh factor reached **OPTIMAL at 85 cumulative
iterations**. The scope is the working snapshot model; this is neither a new
original-model certificate nor reproduction of factor-update drift.

The machine-readable observations are in [F04-results.json](F04-results.json).
They also retain the 16-solve preflight smoke run under its earlier source hash,
separately from final measurements. Raw reports and task-owned replay snapshots
remain local under `/tmp/simplex-F04-*`. No external model is committed, and the
large stress-only inputs were not solved.
