# F12 — Adaptive dual cost perturbation

Status: implementation, complete suites, and measurements verified.
Parent: `076504c` (F11). Adaptive behavior remains opt-in; legacy is the default.

## Behavior

A completed F11 stagnation window can shift near-zero nonbasic costs toward the
interior of the dual-feasible cone. Lower-bound variables receive positive
reduced-cost margins; upper-bound variables receive negative margins. Basic,
fixed, and free variables are excluded. Rational arithmetic receives no shifts.

Margins use the user dual tolerance, a deterministic index multiplier 8–23,
and a local cost scale bounded between one and two. At most three attempts
increase margins by powers of two. Total displacement from the saved original
working cost is capped at 512 dual tolerances. Nonfinite, unrepresentable,
excessively rounded, and infeasible shifts are rejected. A rejected attempt
consumes a level, preventing unlimited retries. Cancellation before publication
leaves costs, prices, and level unchanged.

The lazy journal owns original and active working costs; neither aliases the
input model. Restoration copies saved values directly, including signed zero,
rather than subtracting shifts. BigFloat calculations retain stored workspace
precision. Subsequent pivots and basis recovery update the active working
buffer. Recovery imposes a two-window cooldown while preserving F11 history.
Journal levels survive monitor replacement when working costs change.

Automatic shifts require both the progress monitor and the shared feasibility
recovery driver. Auxiliary phases receive unshifted working costs and cannot
apply adaptive cost perturbations. Original-objective cleanup retires the
journal and restores model costs, including any earlier repair shifts. An
infeasibility proof obtained with an active journal also passes through this
cleanup. Cleanup shares the original time and completed-step budgets; it cannot
certify a shifted objective or silently receive extra iterations.

The internal `adaptive_dual_perturbation` switch supports ablation. Disabling it,
`adaptive_stalling`, or `feasibility_recovery` retains the earlier 1024-zero-step
cost trigger. Public tolerances, statuses, and legacy defaults are unchanged.

the adaptive strategy, 100000 iterations, 60 seconds per solve, and 24576 MiB
(requested; effective allowance 25141338112 bytes). All MIPLib models are explicit
LP relaxations. Each worker performs two full warmups before measured solves.

- pk1 timing: seven samples per algorithm and arm, diagnostics off.
- Frozen degenerate suite: degen2, degen3, pk1, markshare_4_0, and the generated
  degenerate box; one sample per algorithm and arm, diagnostics/kernel timing

  Preset parameters are not tuned using its outcome.
- F11 parent diagnostic observations are reused with explicit labels. They use
  source SHA256 `4bd245033ecef2fe8880595f59fcc30d50f82aa9cc3bd92819a4f922a0150036`.

revision is the earlier HEAD. Their recorded source-tree hash matches

Runtime, medium, the complete quick/holdout suites, the FT timing matrix, and
oversized full solves are outside this feature's measurement queue. Future

## Interpretation and limits

The artifact contains 52 fresh observations and 12 explicitly reused parent
observations: 52 reported OPTIMAL and 12 TIME_LIMIT overall. Every reported
optimum passes the original-model primal check. The fresh observations contain
44 OPTIMAL and 8 TIME_LIMIT. All workers completed within their process resource
budgets. [Raw results](F12-results.json) retain source/data hashes, options,
policies, limits, timings, allocations, diagnostics, and validation metrics.

The paired degenerate census has identical statuses, iteration counts,
refactorizations, and available objectives. No adaptive perturbation occurs in
that suite. Its degen2 dual cleanup event is existing repair-cost cleanup, not
an adaptive perturbation. pk1 timing samples also have identical work counts
(primal 60 steps / 3 refactors; dual 48 / 2). Millisecond timing differences
without changed work do not establish a speed improvement.

The independent 10teams dual run records three perturbation events, three cost
restoration events, three feasibility recoveries and repairs, and two auxiliary
phases. The new trigger is exercised on a corpus LP. The run
still reaches the 60-second limit; it never reaches terminal objective cleanup
or certification. More completed pivots in that censored run are not faster
completion. Increased recovery activity is not evidence of improved stability.
The primal-start path does not enable dual cost perturbation during its
algorithm switches; its different time-limited prefix has no perturbation event.

There is no new solved-model coverage or established general speed or stability
benefit in these observations. Keep adaptive behavior opt-in. The direct solver
integration tests establish restoration and original-LP cleanup on terminating
cases; they do not imply that these limits resolve every degenerate model.

The public solution object does not expose a mapped original-model dual witness.
For some diagnostic runs, the harness can recover one from a captured workspace
whose matrix and objective match the scaled original model. Such a witness is
available in 15 observations here, including five parent observations. Twelve
have zero dual-sign and complementarity errors. In the degen2 dual observation
from the parent and both current arms, the dual-sign error is unchanged at
2.955113931335518e-13 and strict complementarity is infinity. The harness assigns
infinity when any nonzero price points toward a missing bound, even for a tiny
wrong-sign residual. This inherited diagnostic is preserved, not replaced with
a finite value or counted as an independent complementarity certificate.
Other observations retain an explicit unavailable-witness note. These limits
preclude a claim that all optima have independently verified dual certificates.
