# F06: Verified basis checkpoints and repair

The opt-in adaptive profile enables the independently switchable `recovery`
stage. A typed cache keeps at most two recently verified basis checkpoints.
Each owns basis indices/states, working costs and bounds, and perturbation
metadata. Matrix identity, dimensions and phase identity reject unrelated
restores. Checkpoints contain neither elapsed time nor consumed iterations.
Restoring original costs, returning from the auxiliary phase and overlaying a
replay invalidate prior phase checkpoints.

Restoration constructs a fresh factorization and private working arrays;
mutable backend scratch is never borrowed from the live factor. It recomputes
basic values and prices and independently checks normal and transposed solve
quality. Devex references are reset, primal pricing caches are invalidated,
and dual steepest-edge weights are rebuilt from the new inverse. Stored
BigFloat precision covers the entire rebuild, including RHS assembly, rather
than only the LU. Exact rational arithmetic remains exact.

Repair tries at most `max_pivot_candidates` suspect rows and at most
`max_pivot_candidates^2` total one-column trial exchanges. It prefers the row's nonbasic
slack, then other slacks and structural columns. A trial must have a
nonsingular factor and reliable solves. Failed searches try the newest
verified checkpoint and retain bounded pivot-pair rejections for the resulting
basis generation. Rejection checks do not hash the basis on ordinary pivots
when there are no stored rejections.

Callbacks run before publication, after which factor, basis, values, costs,
weights and checkpoint storage are committed without calling user code.
Numerical work still consumes time and factorization counts; no rollback
decreases completed iterations. A deadline after factorization discards the
trial. Logger and callback exceptions preserve their provenance.

After bounded pivot refresh fails, recovery can exchange or restore a basis.
Continuation is bounded by `max_recovery_rounds` and requires the current
method's feasibility invariant. This stage does not implement primal/dual
handoff or an auxiliary feasibility phase: those remain F07. A recovered basis
requiring that work yields a numerical termination at this intermediate stage.
Recovery remains usable when the independent pivot-validation gate is disabled.

## Targeted validation

The missing checkpoint interface failed before implementation. The initial
ownership and restoration checks then passed 44/44 across Float32, Float64,
BigFloat and Rational{BigInt}. Tests exposed missing phase invalidation and
edge-weight reconstruction (11 failures), followed by a stored-precision RHS
regression: rebuilding a 384-bit problem under a 96-bit ambient context lost a
small bound displacement. The corrected rebuild passed 149/149 checks.

A singular basis with two distinct dependent columns demonstrates that fresh
LU alone is insufficient. Bounded column exchange repairs it. A driver test
first failed four assertions, then reached the correct optimum without
changing the consumed iteration count. Repeating with pivot validation disabled
exposed four further failures before recovery was independently connected.
Logger/observer exceptions, timeouts, exhausted searches, restored working
perturbations and generation-scoped rejection tests passed 184/184 checks.

The first combined pivot/refinement/recovery regression run passed 791/791;
JET passed all 26 inference checks. Final review found one lost trigger-pair
assertion and two replay phase-ownership assertions. Those regressions were
added before fixing the boundaries. A separate replay suite passed 10/10.
The corrected combined run passed 794/794 and JET again passed 26/26.
External smoke passed 16/16 OPTIMAL solves with original-primal validation.
The initial full suites passed 226,313/226,313 production and 636/636
development checks. After integrating the trigger-consistency correction,
standard `Pkg.test()` passed **226,321/226,321** checks in 14m50.3s and the
development suite passed **636/636**, including JET. External repeated measurements and both retained replay runs also passed,
as detailed below.

## Replay behavior

`JSimplexReplay.load_snapshot(path)` still requires a successful fresh rebuild.
The explicit `repair_basis=true` keyword permits bounded basis exchange after
a numerical rebuild failure and marks `basis_repaired` in returned metadata.
It preserves both prior counters and new factorization work. A deterministic
singular-basis snapshot is rejected by ordinary loading, then recovers and
reaches its analytic optimum when repair is requested. This is a synthetic
recovery reproducer, not a newly discovered external-model failure.

Parent: `26a648a`. Current tested production-source SHA-256:
`724391e826144e3a789a99ace5e4ce6dd8b693dec7471678c4814ae65c475717`.
The default remains legacy; this intermediate stage remains opt-in.

## Trigger-pair consistency review

An additional reproducer showed that a candidate failing after row selection,
but before choosing an entering column, could combine its new row with the
previous candidate's entering column. For consecutive triggers `(1,1)` and
`(2,0)`, the recorded pair incorrectly became `(2,1)`. This could reject a pivot
that did not cause the failure. Trigger coordinates now move together; only
an entirely unknown `(0,0)` refresh failure retains the preceding trigger.
Eight assertions cover complete, row-only, column-only and unknown triggers.
An isolated prototype passed 802/802 pivot/refinement/recovery checks before
integration. Final full-suite verification uses the integrated correction, with the same
production-source hash as the passing prototype.

## External comparison and performance limits

Original d93cfd3, the F05 adaptive parent, current legacy, current adaptive
with `recovery = false`, and full adaptive each completed **112/112 OPTIMAL**
samples. The eight frozen NetLib/MIPLib cases ran both algorithms seven times
with a 60-second per-solve limit; MIPLib cases were explicit LP relaxations.
Every sample passed original-model primal validation. Objectives agreed with
both references within `max(1e-7, 1e-9*abs(objective))`.

Both full adaptive and the ablation exactly preserved F05 adaptive objectives,
iterations and refactorizations. Current legacy exactly preserved the previous
legacy observations. The quick cases did not exercise a basis repair or
checkpoint restoration, so they measure ordinary operation and checkpoint
maintenance rather than improved external solved-instance coverage.

The geometric mean of per-case median time ratios against F05 adaptive was
**1.061 for primal** and **1.025 for dual**. Against original d93cfd3, the ratios
were **3.130** and **1.772**. The ablation ratios against F05 were 1.031 and 1.003;
individual short-case timings vary, so these differences do not isolate the
cost of checkpoint operations alone. There is no general speedup claim, and
the accumulated adaptive changes do not yet satisfy the final default-rollout
performance gate.

## Diagnosed runs and retained replays

The separate diagnosed quick run completed **16/16 OPTIMAL** solves with
original-primal validation. Primal recorded 50 checkpoint events and 3 rejected
pivots; dual recorded 60 checkpoint events and no rejected pivots. Both recorded
zero basis repairs and zero checkpoint restorations. Corrections, kernel times,
memory observations, source/dataset hashes and nonfinite diagnostic metrics
remain in the machine-readable data. Original-primal validation is not an
independent original-model dual certificate.

A freshly captured actual legacy pk1 repair state replayed with all four
implemented stages enabled reached **OPTIMAL at 85 cumulative iterations**.
Its scope is the working snapshot with a fresh factor, not reproduction of
historical factor-update drift or independent certification of the original LP.

The separate synthetic dependent-column replay demonstrates the F06 repair:
fresh LU fails, explicit repair changes a basis column, and the analytic
optimum is reached at **11 cumulative iterations and 9 refactorizations**:
7 prior, 1 failed fresh rebuild and 1 successful repaired rebuild. This does
not count as recovery of a real late external failure for the F07 milestone.

[F06-results.json](F06-results.json) retains 593 solve observations and both
replays. The 16-solve initial smoke is retained under its earlier source hash;
final comparisons use the corrected source. Raw reports and task-owned replay
files remain under `/tmp/simplex-F06-*`. External data are read-only and are
not committed. No oversized stress-only model was solved.
