# F21 — Reusing verified bases in Phase I

Correctness validation and the four-group benchmark audit passed. The feature
remains opt-in because measured performance is mixed.

## Implementation

The opt-in `phase_one` numerical policy extends the current verified basis instead
of assuming a slack start. A violated basic variable is placed at its violated
bound, and its basis column is replaced by a signed artificial copy. Other basic
values are preserved algebraically. Only violated basic variables get artificials;
the objective remains the sum of artificials. Matrix construction copies sparse
CSC columns without creating a dense artificial matrix. A failed extension has
one verified slack fallback.

The primal Phase I driver shares completed-work and time budgets with original
optimization, uses the existing incremental steps and bounded recovery, and
restores working perturbations before accepting its auxiliary optimum. Phase-I
pricing retains zero reduced-cost tolerance, since a small price can remove a
large primal violation. Auxiliary success returns a feasible original basis;
it does not report an original objective or primal solution as optimal.

Basic artificials leave by actual validated exchanges. Original fixed and free
columns are eligible. Candidate selection scans sparse columns, independent
BTRAN/FTRAN estimates validate the pivot, and a fresh factor checks the exchanged
basis. A small nonzero artificial requires its actual movement and subsequent
original-model feasibility checks. Merely changing an index is insufficient.
Removal steps consume the same iteration budget as optimization. Cancellation
is checked before initial/auxiliary factorization, after recomputation and
certification, and throughout removal.

The original workspace adopts a freshly constructed original-dimension factor,
state, costs, bounds, weights and scratch storage only after verification. Phase
row indices, perturbation journals, pricing storage and checkpoints are retired.
The input problem is unchanged. Stored BigFloat precision is preserved even when
the ambient precision is lower.

The modern auxiliary dual phase now owns the model of its actual working box and
objective, so shared recovery cannot accidentally restore unrelated original
bounds. It uses the shared driver with nested auxiliary construction disabled.
An improving recession direction still requires the existing original-direction
proof and a separate original-feasibility proof before returning `UNBOUNDED`.

A positive artificial optimum is not enough to return `INFEASIBLE`: an
original-model Farkas proof remains mandatory. For contradictory floating
equalities whose proof needs an exactly zero coefficient on an unbounded
variable, interval arithmetic may leave the proof inconclusive; this returns
`NUMERICAL_ERROR`. The corresponding exact rational case can be certified.

## Scope and defaults

`phase_one=false` remains the default in both legacy and adaptive strategies
pending the F25 integration evidence. Existing legacy helpers remain available.
With `phase_one=true`, primal crash initialization may retain a partially feasible
basis for extension; the legacy primal path still requires its existing slack or
fully feasible start.

There are no new production dependencies or public result/status changes.
`run_phase_one!` is an internal feasibility operation: its `OPTIMAL` result has no
objective/primal payload, and the original objective must subsequently be solved
and certified. Uncertain construction, removal, or certificates report numerical
failure; interrupted removal preserves the original numerical state and all
consumed work.

## Validation

The complete production suite passed 251,078/251,078 checks via `Pkg.test()` in
77 minutes 57 seconds. The complete development suite passed 1,066/1,066 checks
across 61 testsets, including 193 JET checks and all worker solve/replay/component
modes. The eight final deadline regressions also passed. Production source was
frozen throughout the successful validation and subsequent measurements.

The production suite took longer than F20 (43 minutes 41 seconds). A single
one-second diagnostic sample identified LLVM compilation of a primal iteration
variant during the long run. This correctness-suite duration, including that
diagnostic sample, is not a warmed solve-time measurement. No test limits or
allocation assertions were relaxed. The benchmark audit also passed.

The development worker dispatches solve, replay and component operations across
inference boundaries. The previous monolithic inference path exceeded a
210-second worker allowance before entering the selected tiny solve; a bounded
reproduction located the compiler work and the corrected worker completed with
the expected numerical-failure sample. Solver limits and test expectations are
unchanged. Parent and modern performance groups use this same worker.

## Measurements

The machine-readable [results](F21-results.json) retain all 168 observations,
including outliers, phase accounting, source/input hashes, options, diagnostics,
and the GLPK reference. All observations are `OPTIMAL`, pass original-model primal
certification, and agree with the GLPK objectives within the audit tolerance.
The GLPK 5.0 reference from F20 is reused only after verifying identical
decompressed input hashes: `flugpl` 1167185.72559232, `stein9inf` 4, generated
phase-one 3. MIPLib models are explicit LP relaxations.

The parent is `67cd4ab` (source fingerprint
`7096d8de83574e5e409b191b7c45ffd905a471f9469a86b00162db13aa3c2f5d`);
F21 uses frozen fingerprint
`35100d1d102f6dfde733ac96fa9bc5bedd5a9e196f7fff1b6156b2a1b6b2d7e6`.
Four sequential groups compare parent/F21 with crash disabled/enabled. Both
algorithms use adaptive policy, automatic pricing, native PFI, presolve disabled,
`refactor_timing=false`, one BLAS thread, two complete solver warmups and seven
recorded samples per case. No correctness jobs ran concurrently.

Each complete solve has a 60-second limit and 100,000-step budget. The process
memory request is 24,576 MiB, clipped to 25,141,338,112 bytes of physical memory.
Peak observed process RSS is 1,639,301,120 bytes, including compilation and warmup.
No memory limit, timeout or incomplete case occurred. This preset excludes
`runtime.mps` and `medium.mps`; their integration runs retain 360-second solve
budgets and the 24 GiB request. No stress-only model was solved or fully factored.

With crash enabled, F21 retains the partially feasible structural basis, whereas
the parent falls back to slack initialization. On `stein9inf`, this reduces Phase
I from 13 to 2 steps and total work from 18 to 10 steps; complete median time falls
from 4.3675 to 0.2993 ms. On `flugpl`, Phase I falls from 13 to 10 steps but original
optimization then needs 10 steps rather than 1. Including the same 10 crash steps,
total work rises from 24 to 30 and median time from 0.8343 to 3.0994 ms. Reusing a
basis is therefore not a universal improvement. Without crash, primal `stein9inf`
also regresses from 4.0700 to 5.6179 ms despite an unchanged pivot count.

The first recorded primal sample in each case/group contains residual observer
compilation (0.13830 seconds summed across the 12 samples), despite the two solver
warmups. Those samples are retained in both the raw data and seven-sample medians;
no recompilation is recorded. They explain the large first-sample maxima and make
these data unsuitable for claims about fully warmed individual samples or cold
startup. The remaining samples have zero recorded compilation. These small cases
support the qualitative mixed-result conclusion, not a broad speedup claim.

Original primal error is at most 3.908e-12 and objective evaluation error at most
1.659e-10. Mapped dual diagnostics are available for 84/168 records. Of those,
63 have finite complementarity error (at most 3.331e-16); 21 `stein9inf` records
report infinity because the diagnostic encounters a nonzero signed price that
requires an absent bound. Their dual sign errors are at most 1.666e-16. The
remaining 84 records have no mapped original dual witness in the public result.
Diagnostic availability must not be read as universal KKT success.

The measured regressions exceed the rollout thresholds for some primal cases.
Both `phase_one` and `crash` remain disabled by default, pending the wider F25
integration comparison. No tolerances, certificates, limits or failed outcomes
were changed to obtain these results.
