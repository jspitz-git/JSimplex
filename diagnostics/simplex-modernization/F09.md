## Behavior

A validated old-basis column updates the basic values and the signed entering
value. The old-basis tableau row updates reduced costs with
`alpha = reduced_cost[entering] / tableau_row[entering]`. The entering reduced
cost becomes zero, the leaving reduced cost becomes `-alpha`, and all remaining
basic reduced costs stay exactly zero. Basis indices, states, and the basic
mask change together. The ratio test supplies the leaving bound state explicitly,
including degenerate zero steps.

Predicted values and prices are checked before factor application. The existing
transactional driver retains deadline checks and publishes the complete step
before completion observers. Audits distinguish inaccurate predictions from
unreliable recomputation. A correction may be published only after the new
basis and recomputed values/prices are independently verified and primal
feasibility holds. An unreliable or infeasible candidate is discarded, rebuilding
the old factor without counting a completed pivot. Stored BigFloat precision
covers the complete incremental iteration.

Devex reuses the independently validated old-basis row. Steepest edge reuses
that same row but retains its distinct auxiliary solve and pricing row. Neither
operation overwrites the entering column before factor application.

Full value/cost recomputation occurs on the existing refactorization path,
phase or working-cost changes, and before terminal certification. Every 20
completed steps, an independent audit checks predicted primal values and
reduced costs. Pricing uses the componentwise residual of
`[A'; -I] * dual + predicted_reduced_costs = working_costs`, evaluated by the
shared numerical-quality machinery without materializing the augmented matrix. Loss of feasibility returns to the F07 driver when enabled.
An independent pivot switch also rechecks primal feasibility after the final
recomputation before considering an unboundedness certificate.

`incremental_primal_pivots=false` disables F09 while retaining F08 bound flips.
Legacy remains the default. Existing validation BTRANs now participate in
kernel instrumentation; saved-solve comparisons therefore use a current-source
ablation rather than older, incomplete raw counters.

## Initial measurement and audit correction

The first implementation passed its full correctness suites and all **576**
measured/census solves, but its quick primal geometric-mean time was **1.263**
times F08 (dual **1.086**). On adlittle it used 143 steps and 54 refactorizations,
versus 115 and 9 for F08; flugpl used 24 steps versus 11 and took 6.206 times
F08. These observations remain in [F09-initial-results.json](F09-initial-results.json).
The initial long-model queue was deliberately held before runtime/greenbea.

An instrumented adlittle reproduction found repeated failures at audit 40:
forward price differences around `1e-12` exceeded a tolerance intended for
backward error despite a reliable, primal-feasible recomputation. The revised
pricing audit uses the original arithmetic terms in its residual scale, with
shared wide-range and stored-precision safeguards. Its regression first failed
five checks and then passed **16/16**; explicit-system, transpose, and stored-
precision comparisons add **31/31**.

A second regression established that verified audit corrections should retain
the new feasible basis instead of undoing a numerically valid pivot. Its RED
had 20 passes and eight failures; GREEN passed **28/28** across all four update
methods. The drift-observer regression checks that correction path;
separate fault-injection tests retain rollback requirements for nonfinite and
infeasible recomputed candidates. F08's standalone flip rollback remains intact.
No user tolerance or shared numerical error threshold changed.

Diagnostic checks on the corrected code returned optimal original-primal-valid
solutions for adlittle (127 steps, 9 refactorizations, one rejection), flugpl
(24/3, zero rejections), and pk1 (60/3, zero rejections). These checks establish
correctness and work counts; the repeated measurements below provide the timing evidence.

Difficult-model statuses are reported as observed; a time limit is not a
mathematical failure certificate. The harness adds independent original-
primal validation when a solution is returned. Dual diagnostic fields
retain the availability limitations documented in the earlier reports.

The corrected quick measurements do **not** establish a global speedup. F09
reduces primal time against its current-source ablation, while the parent
comparison is slightly slower overall and flugpl remains about 3.17 times
slower. Even the disabled-feature ablation is 1.159 times the parent in primal
and 1.184 in dual, despite identical objectives and work counts. These
sequential profile measurements cannot attribute the entire time difference
to the new algorithm. Adaptive remains opt-in; the F10 primal-cost milestone
still requires separate evaluation. Across the eight primal census models,
FTRAN counts fall from 666 to 517 and BTRAN counts from 1,376 to 841, despite
the total completed-step count increasing from 285 to 322. Flugpl is the
exception: its longer pivot path increases both solve counts.

Both runtime profiles reach the 360-second limit. F09 completes 458 steps
versus 432 for F08, but also performs 440 versus 388 refactorizations and
rejects 1,661 versus 1,466 pivots. This does not establish a solved model or
an improvement in numerical stability.

Greenbea also reaches the 60-second limit in both profiles. F09 records fewer
refactorizations (113 versus 418) and rejected pivots (9 versus 38), but fewer
completed steps (868 versus 1,464). These incomplete, different pivot paths
do not establish faster completion or additional solved coverage.

The F08 runtime profile spends only 1.237 seconds in the instrumented raw
refactorization kernel, despite 388 refactorizations and 360.186 seconds
overall. Existing kernel timers exclude substantial residual/refinement and
other work, so these counters alone cannot identify the dominant cost.

Whole-solve runtime comparisons use a 360-second limit and a 24 GiB allowance.
The benchmark harness performs separate warmups before its measured solve;
a limit-censored diagnostic profile can therefore take about 18 minutes.
MIPLib inputs are explicit LP relaxations. The three oversized stress-only
models remain excluded from complete solves.
