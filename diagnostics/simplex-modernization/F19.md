# F19: Adaptive hypersparse simplex pipeline

Status: verified opt-in implementation; all preset measurements and the final
artifact audit completed. Parent: `84983c3` (F18). Source fingerprint:
`1c7c6dfda121785fbe59b0ad20213b85458ac627f1397a181e201af987e506b3`.

## Behavior

The opt-in `hypersparse=true` policy now routes primal/dual basis solves, pricing,
BFRT aggregate right-hand sides and edge-weight work through owned support
metadata. The six existing scratch value vectors remain the numerical storage;
the pipeline does not copy dense values between each solve and pricing stage.
Unit, entering-column, BFRT and primal-weight producers assemble support directly.
Refinement, residual scratch and high-precision recovery writes invalidate it.
Candidate values/support are independent; mode observations belong to the live
phase. Working-model/phase invalidation clears the cache recursively.

Each of FTRAN, BTRAN, pricing, BFRT, weight FTRAN and weight BTRAN has independent
selection state. The experimental thresholds are 0.1/0.2 occupancy, incorporating
the preceding output density. A change needs two consecutive indications. The
median of the last three successful complete operations estimates each kernel's
cost; unobserved, nonpositive and nonfinite costs supply no evidence. Every 32nd
eligible low-density call substitutes the alternative kernel to refresh its cost.
It does not perform a duplicate solve. Empty inputs have an explicit fast path.
Unavailable adapters select the ordinary backend. F18's internal density fallback
still handles fill arising within a selected sparse solve.

BigFloat branches retain stored factor, matrix and RHS precision. Exact rational
operations never convert to floating-point arithmetic. Internal arithmetic
overflow reaches the existing numerical recovery path; caller exceptions retain
their identity. Residual checks and original-model certification remain enabled.
Automatic working-type transfer belongs to F22/F23; F19 covers the existing
high-precision repair hooks and stored-precision checkpoint reconstruction.

Both public strategies retain `hypersparse=false`. When explicitly enabled, the
pipeline takes precedence over standalone F16 `sparse_pricing`. Transactional
scratch copies, residual checks and some vector consumers still traverse dense
storage; this feature does not make an entire simplex iteration proportional to
support size.

## Accounting

Operation timing includes preparation, graph extraction and support conversion.
Optional diagnostic sub-timers expose setup, base/upper graphs and support rebuilds;
they overlap operation totals and must not be added as disjoint work. Failed
numerical work remains in attempted-iteration accounting. F10 continues to measure
complete iterations and prioritize numerical quality over economic heuristics.

Factor storage counts now include active, owned coefficient snapshots (graph
matrices, diagonals, scaling and any copied dense core). A fresh factor's baseline
is its actual storage before lazy extraction, so activating a cache consumes real
fill headroom. Retired buffers and numerical work vectors are not factor entries.
Component reports separately record reachable cache bytes; borrowed model/value
arrays and shared read-only graphs prohibit adding those sizes across workspaces.

## Preset verification protocol

The following selection is fixed before collecting performance observations:

- Targeted pipeline, recovery, inference and allocation tests, related simplex,
  factor, refinement and refactorization regressions, then full production and
  development suites. No performance experiment runs concurrently with them.
- `sparse_large` and `holdout`, PFI/native, both algorithms, adaptive/auto pricing,
  hypersparse enabled and disabled, `refactor_timing=false`, one recorded solve
  after separate full warmups of the uninstrumented and instrumented solver,
  60 seconds per solve and 100000 completed steps.
- Full eligible `runtime.mps` and `medium.mps` runs under the same two policies
  and algorithms, with **360 seconds per solve** and the same step budget.
  Each algorithm has both warmups and one measured solve; both algorithms can
  therefore consume 36 minutes per configuration, plus loading/compilation.
- All LP workers request 24576 MiB. The enforced address-space allowance is
  clipped to physical memory by the existing runner; peak RSS is separate.
  MIP inputs are explicitly LP-relaxed. Censored/failing outcomes remain visible.
- A fixed 1024-dimensional, 2944-entry generated fixture compares RHS assembly,
  BTRAN, pricing and FTRAN across baseline, forced sparse and automatic pipeline
  paths, all four updates and both backends. Each of 72 profiles has seven samples
  of 100 complete pipelines. Setup, first use, candidate copy and cache storage
  are separate. The candidate-copy observation is its first call in that setup
  and may include compilation; it is not a warmed steady-state copy estimate.
  This fixed-basis experiment is not an end-to-end LP speed score.
- Bounded components from `scsd8`, `markshare_4_0`, `runtime`, `medium` and `largo`
  retain the existing effective 60-second component ceiling and respective
  64/64/64/128/160 MiB parser quotas. One-MiB quota probes cover all three excluded
  names; prefix inspection covers `big` and `AnyMOD`. No excluded model reaches
  full-sized factorization or an end-to-end solve.

## Results

Targeted pipeline tests and inference checks passed **3009/3009**. Related
numerical regressions passed 11703 checks; the final full JET suite passed 169,
for 11872 related checks. CLI/component/policy checks passed 96/96. The earlier
full-JET failure was retained locally and fixed before freezing: nested timing
closures defeated deep inference, so pipeline sub-timers now use explicit
callback-free start/finish scopes. The original numerical regressions were not
repeated for this instrumentation-only correction.

The warm allocation guard allows at most 4096 bytes for 64 complete pipelines at
dimension 1024, below one full vector copy. Initial failures exposed repeated
matching and precision closures; the corrected implementation passes without
raising the limit. Separate regressions cover fixed-width rational subtraction
at `typemin(Int64)` and stored BigFloat weight coefficients under lower ambient
precision. No numerical tolerance was weakened.

The first full development run stopped at an existing CLI completion test: its
30-second cold-worker deadline expired during LLVM compilation of the new bounded
pipeline probe. An isolated reproduction confirmed the same termination. That
component-completion test now uses the runner's existing 60-second component cap;
reader, quota, and LP solve budgets are unchanged. The failed log is retained.
The complete development rerun passed **1025/1025 checks in 59 testsets**, including
the component-completion test and all 169 JET checks.

The frozen-source production suite passed **249753/249753 checks in 39m34.9s**.
All 19 preset report groups completed, and the final audit verified their source,
runner and input fingerprints, policy/limits, original primal checks, 504 kernel
timings, and bounded component dimensions. `git diff --check` passed.

The parser rejected the extended `NAME` header in the supplied `blend.mps` in both

two input failures are not successful or timed LP solves.

The enabled primal run on `fast0507` returned `NUMERICAL_ERROR` after 35.8123 s,
4024 steps, and 583 refactorizations. Bounded feasibility recovery was exhausted
both initially and on the original-model retry; certification failed twice.
The disabled run reached its 60-second limit at 4398 steps. This adverse result

improvement. No tolerance, recovery bound, or preset was changed in response.

`nw04` reported `OPTIMAL` in both modes and both algorithms, with original-model

16310.6666666667 throughout. `share2b` and the `air03` LP relaxation also reported

1.1893 s versus 2.9222 s, while enabled dual `share2b` took 0.3915 s versus 0.2167 s.

All twelve `OPTIMAL` observations expose mapped dual-error diagnostics here.

### Fixed-basis pipeline observations

All 72 profiles passed their solve/pricing reference checks and produced seven
samples each, giving 504 measured batches of 100 pipelines. The following ranges
compare each profile's median with its corresponding ordinary dense baseline
across the four update implementations and two backends; smaller is faster.

| RHS support | Forced sparse / baseline | Automatic / baseline |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.127–0.261 | 0.159–0.265 |
| 16 | 0.825–2.020 | 0.824–1.355 |
| 1024 | 2.170–2.711 | 1.552–1.756 |

The automatic path has measurable dense-input overhead even when its individual
operations choose dense fallbacks. Forced sparse kernels remain an experimental
comparison, not a recommended universal path. All warmed baseline and automatic
samples allocated zero bytes. Forced sparse samples also allocated zero bytes
for supports 1 and 1024, but support 16 allocated 2312–2319.04 bytes per pipeline.
Those allocations are retained; the unit-support allocation guard does not claim
zero allocation for every support shape.

Separate fresh workspace setup observations spanned 0.460–8.598 ms and roughly
2.34–2.65 MB allocated. First forced-sparse pipelines cost 115.2–227.0 microseconds
and allocated 729448–937200 bytes; lazy graph/index setup is included. First
automatic calls may still select dense kernels, so their 18.6–34.8 microseconds
and 1456 allocated bytes are not the complete eventual cache-activation cost.
The first baseline candidate-copy calls included compilation (117.8–163.0 ms,
roughly 17–21.3 MB), whereas later sparse/automatic observations were 24.6–40.2
microseconds and about 301 KB. These first-call numbers are not comparable warmed
copy benchmarks and do not support a copy-speed claim.

At unit support, active coefficient storage grew from 2956–4992 entries for the
baseline to 10013–14080 for the enabled paths. Factor caches occupied
302912–369536 reachable bytes, and pipeline caches 245312 reachable bytes.
Borrowed model/value storage and shared read-only graphs prevent adding these
figures across workspaces. Setup/copy observations are shared by each setup's
three support profiles; there were 24 setups, not 72 independent setup samples.

### Scope of the result

The completed LP experiments contain 52 recorded solves: 12 `OPTIMAL`, 39
`TIME_LIMIT`, and one `NUMERICAL_ERROR`, plus the two parser failures without solve
samples. The sparse-unit kernel benefit is clear on the fixed fixture, but LP
results are mixed and all eight 360-second observations remain unfinished.
`hypersparse=false` therefore remains the default for both public strategies.
No result justifies a general speed or stability improvement claim. Full raw
observations are retained in [F19-results.json](F19-results.json).
