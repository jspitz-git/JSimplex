Status: implementation, complete test suites, and measurements are verified.
Adaptive behavior remains opt-in; the broad primal-cost milestone is unmet.
Parent: `c9a7786` (F09).

## Behavior

Both simplex methods use the same internal refactorization state when
`adaptive_refactor` is enabled. Unreliable solve quality precedes guarded
factor growth, active storage growth, the hard update ceiling, and economic
decisions. Missing quality is not treated as a numerical failure. Rational
arithmetic retains its configured fixed interval; the legacy dual interval
logic remains separate.

The initial interval comes from `refactorization_interval`. Three reliable,
productive cycles permit bounded growth; repeated failures shorten the
interval. Growth/fill rebuilds do not earn healthy-cycle growth. A cycle is
credited only after its replacement LU succeeds. Failed factorization and
unreliable solve results reset the healthy history.

The economic estimate samples complete attempted simplex steps, including
pricing, row/column solves, BFRT RHS work, and residual/refinement checks.
Nested iteration scopes produce one observation. Steps containing a factor
rebuild are excluded from the solve-cost EMA because LU has a separate
estimate. Failed attempts retain sampled elapsed work without becoming
successful solve samples. These wall times can include ordinary iteration
overhead and enabled observers; they are heuristic evidence, never a
numerical certificate.

Fresh steps are sampled when available; updated steps and LU work are sampled
at bounded frequencies. Economics requires multiple usable observations and
a cumulative duration floor. The approximate linear-overhead model compares
the observed extra step cost with twice the amortized LU cost at the current
update age. A hard ceiling applies regardless of measured cost. Zero,
negative, nonfinite, or insufficient observations do not justify an economic
decision.

Active numeric storage is counted for all four update methods and both
backends. Growth/storage metrics are refreshed on the first update, every
eight updates, and at the current interval. They exclude retired buffers and
spare factorizations and do not replace the benchmark memory watchdog. F19
can refine these estimates using sparse reach information.

Staged candidates share the state of their shared factor. Rollback resets
factor age and metrics while retaining attempted-work history. Auxiliary
workspaces own independent state; an active timing scope is not copied into
them. Checkpoint recovery creates state for its fresh factor.

`refactor_timing=false` disables clock-based decisions and sampling. Snapshot
replay enforces this deterministic mode and initializes from the configured
interval; it does not fabricate historical cost samples. Original snapshot
policy metadata is retained. The benchmark CLI now accepts `--basis-update`
to compare PFI and triangular updates with the same harness.

## Interpretation and milestone

The quick corpus does not establish a broad algorithmic speedup. The disabled
current-source ablation is itself faster than the parent on these sequential
measurements despite identical work counts. Therefore the full adaptive/parent
difference cannot be attributed to the new scheduler. Relative to the original
baseline, the combined adaptive PFI path still takes **2.838× primal / 1.964×
dual** time in the quick geometric mean.

PFI retains identical quick work counts. Its primal census contains one fill
trigger and no economic triggers. The Forrest–Tomlin primal census records one
economic trigger and saves three steps, four FTRANs, and seven BTRANs compared
with its ablation, while dual work is unchanged. Timing-enabled repeated paths
vary for `adlittle` dual (109 steps/6 refactors or 101/13) and `pk1` primal
(60/3 or 57/3); deterministic replay disables this source of variation.

All four runtime observations are censored by the solver time limit, not the
memory watchdog. The effective memory allowance is **25,141,338,112 bytes**.
PFI completes more steps than its retained parent reference, whereas
Forrest–Tomlin completes fewer. Both adaptive methods rebuild more often and
reject fewer proposed pivots; neither establishes time to solution, a new
certified solution, or a general stability improvement.

Adaptive runtime records five economic and 185 residual-triggered rebuilds for
PFI, and four economic and 183 residual-triggered rebuilds for Forrest–Tomlin.
Raw LU time remains only 1.9–2.4 seconds out of 360 seconds in these adaptive
observations. It cannot account for most elapsed work; reducing LU counts alone
would not address that remaining cost. The cost sampler therefore measures
complete attempted iterations, with the limitations described above.

F08/F09 reduce basis-solve work, and long-chain numerical tests pass, but cheaper
complete primal solves have not been established for the combined adaptive
path. The F10 primal-cost milestone remains unmet. Keep the feature opt-in and
continue the approved stagnation/pricing work; do not change production defaults
or describe this stage as a successful broad performance rollout.
