## Behavior

After a verified basis recomputation, the shared driver selects the next action:

| Primal feasible | Dual feasible | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes | Yes | Certify |
| Yes | No | Primal simplex |
| No | Yes | Dual simplex |
| No | No | Existing auxiliary phase |

The explicitly requested algorithm still chooses the initial construction.
Adaptive runs can subsequently change methods after basis recovery or loss of
feasibility. The driver preserves the zero reduced-cost tolerance used by primal
Phase I. It reuses the current auxiliary algorithms; F21 remains responsible for
modernizing Phase I.

`SimplexRunBudget` retains the common monotonic start time, remaining limit, and
completed work across method changes, cleanup, and replacement workspaces.
Iteration and refactorization offsets distinguish outer retries from inner
phases, so public results add previous work exactly once. Auxiliary work is
retained even when a user callback throws. Rejected candidates do not consume
completed steps.

The driver allows at most two recovery rounds without a completed step, with bounded
signatures of the basis,
nonbasic states, working costs/bounds, and phase. Final objective cleanup also
has at most two continuations. Working costs are restored before accepting
optimality or unboundedness. Infeasibility and unboundedness proofs require the
original bounds; altered working-bound proofs return a numerical status until
F13 supplies bound restoration. Primal-selected and strict Phase I handoffs disable degeneracy
cost shifts; cleanup disables new degeneracy perturbations. Existing original
feasibility, optimality, recession, and postsolve checks remain the status gates.

The new independent `feasibility_recovery` policy switch enables an F07 ablation
that retains F03–F06. The default public strategy remains `legacy`.

## Limits

Internal solve quality is a backward-error check, not a forward-error bound.
Recovery exhaustion returns a numerical status rather than an infeasibility
proof. Callback and logger exceptions retain their provenance. No relaxed
feasibility tolerance or new status shortcut was introduced.

The diagnostic complementarity field can be infinite even for certified solves,
as already recorded in F01–F06. Do not interpret that field as an independent
dual certificate or infer a witness where none is available. Public optimal
results still pass the solver's original-model certification and the harness's
independent original-primal validation.
