# F18 — Indexed support through basis updates

Status: complete; correctness gates passed and all preset observations were audited. Parent: `7e9523d`.

## Behavior

Indexed forward and transpose solves now cover PFI, Forrest–Tomlin, Suhl–Suhl,
and Bartels–Golub histories. PFI transpose uses the entire eta dot product and
reverses history. Triangular updates retain their individual rotation, swap,
and elimination order. Only exact zeros disappear from support.

Each factor lazily owns solve scratch and a cached base adapter. Copies share
read-only coefficients and own all mutable scratch. Successful refactorization
clears the cache; rejected refactorization retains it. A triangular replacement
invalidates its upper graph, rebuilt on demand in O(nnz(U)) time. This extraction
and cache storage are real costs, reported separately from warmed solves.

Internal indexed calls accept `kernel_mode=:sparse`, `:dense`, or `:auto`.
The experimental auto rule materializes dense work above half occupancy,
including intermediate support growth. It is a deterministic F18 fallback;
F19 owns measured-cost selection and hysteresis. Unavailable base adapters use
the existing backend. BigFloat retains stored precision; rationals stay exact.
Numerical overflow raises `OverflowError` and leaves scratch reusable.

Ordinary simplex iterations still use Vector solves. This feature establishes
update support; the suite below is regression evidence, not an end-to-end
speedup measurement of indexed simplex.

## Preset measurements

All numerical measurements start after both full correctness suites finish.
The F01 `sparse_large` suite runs both algorithms for all four update methods,
with one observation after warmup, adaptive strategy, automatic pricing,
`refactor_timing=false`, 100000 completed steps, 60 seconds per solve, and
24576 MiB (clipped to host memory). Seven cases give at most 56 observations;
resource stops and failures remain in the report. MIPLib is explicitly relaxed.
`medium.mps` uses the full memory allowance. `runtime.mps` is not in this suite;
its later complete runs receive at least 360 seconds.

A fixed 1024-dimensional, 2944-entry base fixture compares four and eight
updates, with the latter restoring the explicit basis. All four update methods
and both backends run seven setup samples and six RHS/system profiles. Each
profile compares existing dense, indexed, auto, and indexed plus materialization
paths with seven samples of 100 calls. Setup, update, graph/cache construction,
first indexed solve, copy costs, and active storage are separate. The worker
has 240 seconds and the same memory allowance. Independent direct solves of
the explicitly updated basis check results.

Five bounded external probes use scsd8, markshare, runtime, medium, and largo,
with parser quotas of 64, 64, 64, 128, and 160 MiB. The requested worker
limit is 180 seconds; the existing stress runner caps component workers at
60 seconds, which is the effective deadline.
Update sequences use synthetic normalized component bases of at most 32 rows;
they do not solve the original LP. Cold component timings may include compilation.
Three one-MiB quota checks and two one-MiB prefix inspections cover the excluded
files. No complete solve or full-sized factorization of big, largo, or AnyMOD
is permitted. These checks are excluded from solved coverage and speed scores.

### Generated update kernels

All 16 method/backend/history groups passed their independent direct-solve
references. The 2688 recorded call timings all reported zero allocated bytes
per warmed call. The 112 recorded setup samples retain fresh factorization,
updates, combined base/upper cache construction, first indexed solve, and copy
costs. Upper-only extraction time is not isolated in this experiment.

Inverse column assignment restores the mathematical basis but retains four
explicit stored zeros: the restored CSC has 2948 stored entries versus the
initial 2944. An untimed reconstruction confirmed exact matrix equality and
identical hashes after removing those explicit zeros. Raw storage hashes are
therefore intentionally different; no numerical tolerance was relaxed.

The following seven-sample medians use the four-update history and a unit RHS.
Each cell contains existing dense / indexed / indexed with input-output
materialization times in microseconds. The raw report also retains auto mode,

Across both histories and systems, unit-RHS indexed medians were 6.4–19.4%
of their dense baselines; including conversion they were 8.0–30.0%. This
advantage is confined to the generated sparse profiles. With 16 RHS entries,
indexed time ranged from 0.82 to 2.73 times dense time. With a full RHS, forced
indexed execution was 5.38–11.71 times slower. Auto mode avoided that large
penalty but still took 1.07–1.33 times the original dense-call time.

The native Bartels–Golub grown/unit-transpose samples were visibly noisy:
dense 9.182–22.476 µs, indexed 0.705–2.904 µs, converted 1.449–3.665 µs.
They are retained unchanged. The converted median below the indexed median
in that profile should not be interpreted as evidence of a conversion speedup.

Across the 16 groups, median fresh factorization cost was 279.6–988.5 µs;
history construction 3.5–107.9 µs; combined cache construction 75.2–207.3 µs;
first indexed solve after cache construction 1.8–5.7 µs; and copying an active
cached factor 2.6–44.3 µs. Cache construction allocated 661048–868768 bytes;
the first indexed solve allocated 2832–3824 bytes to grow private traversal
buffers; copying allocated 83808–322912 bytes. Active caches had reachable
storage of 345600–433968 bytes, and factors retained 2964–5000 numeric entries.
Reachable cache sizes include shared read-only data and must not be summed
across copies as if all bytes were newly allocated.

These results support preserving a dense fallback and accounting for setup
and support growth. They do not establish an end-to-end simplex improvement.
In particular, rebuilding a triangular upper graph after a pivot has a real
cost that a warmed repeated-solve measurement alone cannot assess.
