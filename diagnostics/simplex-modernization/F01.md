## Benchmark protocol

Initial ordinary benchmarks use explicit LP relaxation, Float64, unchanged solver
defaults, and a 4 GiB address-space screening limit. Following the user's
clarification that WSL has 24 GB, medium is rerun with `--memory-limit-mib=24576`
(clipped to OS-reported total memory). The earlier address-space limit does
not measure exhaustion of WSL physical RAM. Reports retain source/data hashes,
options, sample times, allocations, peak process RSS, status, iterations,
refactorizations, and available original-unit errors. Instrumented reports also
retain event counts, recent history, observer time, and phase time.

The quick suite contains eight frozen NetLib/MIPLib LPs, both algorithms,
seven measured fresh solves after warmup, a 60-second per-solve deadline, and
100,000 iterations. Run it against the extracted original source and F01 with

## Interpretation limits

Diagnostics remain opt-in. Kernel timings cover the main iteration kernels,
not every auxiliary solve. Fresh-factor replay does not reproduce update-chain
drift; bounded traces retain working factor/update states and may truncate.
Native UMFPACK serialization may rebuild its base factor state. Replay status
refers to the working snapshot LP, not a newly certified original input.

Original dual errors are unavailable when a compatible witness cannot be
mapped. A tiny multiplier toward an absent bound can yield infinite raw
complementarity error; its finite sign violation is reported separately.
No rounding or relaxed certification is used to hide this effect. In the
JSON report, nonfinite values are explicit strings (`"inf"`, `"-inf"`, `"nan"`)
because JSON cannot encode nonfinite numbers.
