A is accepted. All 180 BigFloat cells have lower median paired time (range 0.853617–0.972851), bytes and events. Positive/negative/mixed direction cases all benefit because their ratio steps are positive. At n=16/64, sparse/dense, removed events scale with active rows: 48–768 fewer events, corresponding here to 12 events per active row across the three zero sites. Bytes fall by 1,344–27,648 per call. The 64/128/256-bit contexts all preserve the effect. Negative/zero-step fallbacks were correctness tested but are not a dedicated performance regime; their added comparison cannot be declared free from these timings.

B is accepted as removal of unnecessary allocations. Positive ordinary steps remove eight events and 224–288 bytes, mixed steps remove four and 112–144 bytes, negative steps remove neither. This matches the two, one, or zero unused positive-operand negations. The same event pattern occurs at first observation and window end. Whole-workspace savings are diluted by existing context/scanning work; 62/180 cell timing ratios exceed 1, and 21/180 have higher median bytes (up to 80) despite no event increases. The worst workspace paired median is 1.376170, without GC. There is no reliable whole-workspace speed claim for B.

C is accepted. First/ordinary progress paths have zero byte/event deltas, as expected because the improvement method is not reached. At window end, positive/mixed metrics remove 24 events and negative cases 16; byte decreases are 448–864. All 45 end cells have lower bytes/events; one has time ratio 1.025641. Workspace event decreases are 20 for positive/mixed and 16 for negative shapes, with all 180 workspace cells reducing bytes. Nevertheless 43/180 workspace timing ratios exceed 1; the worst is 1.568182 without GC. Timing cannot be presented as a uniform workspace gain.

BC composition is accepted independently of adding separate timing claims. Its exact progress and workspace event savings are the sum of B and C for matching contexts/shapes. End-progress event reductions are 32/16/28 for positive/negative/mixed and workspace reductions 28/16/24. All 180 workspace cells have lower median bytes/events; 34 have time ratios above 1 (maximum 1.041919). Combined end-progress timing is favorable in all 45 cases. No arithmetic, precision or branch-trajectory changes were needed to obtain these savings.

Native and exact controls are preserved, not suppressed. Every control cell has unchanged median event counts. Float32/64 byte deltas are exactly zero; Rational{BigInt} byte deltas range -112 to +32 and Rational{Int64} -16 to +64. Overall median time/bytes/events ratios for each control type are 1.0. Small Float32 first-observation timings give 2.0 ratios for B/BC while counts remain zero; tiny kernel timings are visibly quantized. The byte variation also exists on unchanged exact-type arithmetic, so it does not establish a new production allocation mechanism. Its cause was not isolated; do not claim byte-perfect invariance or broad speedups from these controls.

All slow cells and all seven original GC windows are preserved in result-review-analysis.json and original raw data. Four GC windows are in original arms and three in candidate arms. No trimming or GC subtraction is used. In particular BF64 nearest/n64 sparse negative C workspace (1.568182) and BF128 down/n64 dense mixed B workspace (1.376170) have highly variable individual times without GC and remain visible. These observations limit the timing conclusion even where allocation work is predictably reduced. The full summary.json retains every cell, and result-review-analysis.json provides group counts/ranges and all slower cells.

## Computational work and retained memory

No unnecessary new vector/matrix scans, basis solves, copies, conversions, invariant loops or retained caches were found. Ratio costs remain proportional to the existing candidate passes; workspace/context scans are retained rather than excluded from the full observer measurement. Allocation-event reductions agree with this source explanation; they are not inferred from iteration counts. Timed functions start from separately reset fixtures, with initialization, compilation, cold/warm work and validation accounted outside measured windows. No whole-solver numerical trajectory or recovery timing claim is supported by this experiment.
