Representative sparse/dense before/after kernel benchmarks are unnecessary here because production is unchanged and no performance improvement is claimed; coverage is this single sparse Float64 basis/RHS only.

The evidence supports why the first weight RHS is refused and why its one correction is discarded. It does not establish full forward accuracy, pivot safety, an LP certificate, all-RHS behavior, or a production fix. The old failed 217-control benchmark remains rejected; this review supplies no waiver.
